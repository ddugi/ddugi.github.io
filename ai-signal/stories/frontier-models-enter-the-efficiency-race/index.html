The frontier model is moving down the cost curve.

Anthropic released Opus 5.5 with lower token prices and provider-reported efficiency gains. The useful comparison is cost per completed task, including the controls around it.

By Dugi SelmanajPublished 22 Sep 2026Updated 25 Sep 20263 min read

01 / Evidence boundary

Verified facts

  • Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 on 22 September 2026. [anthropic-opus-55]
  • Anthropic lists Opus 5.5 at $4 per million input tokens, $20 per million output tokens, and $0.20 per million cache-read tokens, compared with $5, $25, and $0.50 for Opus 5. [anthropic-opus-55]
  • Anthropic reports that Opus 5.5 costs 40% less than Opus 5 on its typical-workload tests and generates output more than 30% faster. [anthropic-opus-55]
  • Anthropic says Frontier Design and METR evaluated the model before release and that biology and cybersecurity safeguards route some work through restricted access or another model. [anthropic-opus-55] [anthropic-opus-55-system-card]
  • Anthropic states that Opus 5.5 may recognize evaluation settings and that pre-deployment evaluations cannot reliably catch every failure. [anthropic-opus-55-system-card]
Reporting context

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 with new pricing, performance claims, a system card, external pre-release evaluations, and capability-linked safeguards.

02 / Observed change

What actually changed

Anthropic moved its top Opus tier to lower input, output, and cache-read prices while reporting higher speed and fewer tokens per task. It released the model with a system card, outside pre-release evaluators, and restricted paths for some biology and cybersecurity work.

03 / Interpretation

Why it matters

Frontier capability becomes easier to use when the same result needs fewer model calls, fewer tokens, and less waiting. That changes routing decisions: teams can reserve an expensive model for hard tasks only if they can measure which tasks actually benefit. The safety controls matter to the same calculation because a fallback or access check can change both result quality and operating cost.

04 / Engineering analysis

Technical implications

Evaluate complete workflows rather than benchmark scores or token prices. Record task success, retries, tool calls, cache use, latency, safeguard interventions, human review, and total spend. A model that charges less per token can still cost more per useful result if it explores longer or needs more correction.

05 / Potential beneficiaries & pressure

Business implications

Lower listed prices put pressure on the premium-model tier and may make advanced models practical for more production work. Procurement still needs a representative task set and independent logs; Anthropic's typical workload is not automatically the buyer's workload.

06 / Possibility, not certainty

Where this could be going

Conditional outlook · Not a fact

Conditional outlook: if independent evaluations confirm lower cost per accepted task, frontier-model competition may shift from peak scores toward efficiency and controllability. If the gains depend on selected harnesses, caching patterns, or provider-defined tasks, the price change will be less structural than the launch suggests.

07 / What could change the picture

Risks & uncertainties

  • Anthropic selected and reported most of the performance, cost, and safety comparisons. External evaluators participated, but their full findings are not all contained in the launch page.
  • The 40% typical-workload figure mixes listed prices with provider-observed token use. Different prompts, tools, cache hit rates, and effort settings can produce a different result.
  • Benchmark margins are small or setup-dependent in several comparisons, and Anthropic itself says those margins are becoming less reliable guides to real-world differences.
  • The system card says the model may recognize evaluations, which weakens confidence that simulated behavior fully predicts unattended production behavior.
08 / Editorial analysis

The AI Signal take

The interesting number is not a leaderboard point. It is the bill for a task someone accepts. Opus 5.5 suggests the frontier is starting to compete on that number, but the receipt still needs retries, safeguards, and human checking included.

09 / Provenance

Sources & publication record

Primary release materials from Anthropic. Price points are public list prices; performance, efficiency, and safety results remain provider-reported unless an external evaluator publishes its own result.

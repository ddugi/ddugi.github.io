Research / Open observation infrastructure for AI weather systemsImportantReported signal

The forecast opened its raw materials.

Google DeepMind has released the live feeds and historical archives behind two important parts of WeatherNext 3: a stitched global satellite view and an AI-derived precipitation record. The signal is bigger than one weather model. A reusable observation layer is becoming public infrastructure for other models to inspect, challenge, and build on.

01 / Evidence boundary

Verified facts

  • Google DeepMind announced on 13 November 2026 that the SatWeave and PARDIG live feeds and historical archives are openly available for weather, hydrology, and climate applications. [deepmind-weather-data]
  • SatWeave combines observations from six geostationary satellites into a global mosaic with 17 visible, water-vapor, and infrared channels; Google says the feed updates hourly and the archive spans more than a decade. [deepmind-weather-data]
  • PARDIG is trained on sparse radar observations from NASA's Global Precipitation Measurement Core Observatory, using SatWeave imagery and ECMWF atmospheric fields as inputs. [deepmind-weather-data] [pardig-paper]
  • The released PARDIG data has 0.1-degree spatial and 15-minute temporal resolution. Google reports average near-real-time latency of about five hours and a historical record extending to 1999. [deepmind-weather-data] [pardig-paper]
  • The authors evaluated PARDIG against radar networks in the United States, Europe, and Japan and more than 36,000 weather stations. They report stronger detection than several global baselines, with the largest improvements at extreme intensities. [deepmind-weather-data] [pardig-paper]
  • The PARDIG paper was submitted to arXiv on 10 November 2026 and states that both historical and near-real-time PARDIG datasets are available under CC BY 4.0; the public dataset announcement followed on 13 November. [pardig-paper] [deepmind-weather-data]
Reporting context

A flood-model team no longer has to begin with a finished forecast or rebuild a global satellite mosaic from incompatible feeds. It can start with the same hourly observation layer and precipitation target used by a production weather model, then test where that layer works—and where it does not.

02 / Observed change

What actually changed

The observation and training-data layer behind a prominent AI forecast system is now available as a live feed and a long historical record. That lets other teams work below the polished forecast output: they can train different models, compare methods, study failure modes, and reuse the inputs for adjacent Earth-science problems.

03 / Interpretation

Why it matters

AI weather progress is often presented as a model race. This release points to a quieter constraint: coherent, timely global observations are infrastructure. Opening that layer makes some claims reproducible and creates a common substrate for flood, drought, energy, and climate work. It also exposes the data assumptions that a model headline can hide.

04 / Engineering analysis

Technical implications

Researchers can ingest an hourly 17-channel satellite mosaic and a 15-minute precipitation product without independently reconciling every source format and projection. They still need to validate regional bias, latency, resolution, missing observations, licensing, storage cost, and fitness for each operational decision. A 10-kilometer precipitation field is not a substitute for local radar or gauges where those are available.

05 / Potential beneficiaries & pressure

Business implications

Weather-sensitive organizations gain a lower-friction input for experimentation, but not an operational guarantee. Teams considering flood response, renewable-energy forecasting, insurance, or logistics should distinguish an open research dataset from a service-level agreement and require local validation before consequential use.

06 / Possibility, not certainty

Where this could be going

Conditional outlook · Not a fact

Conditional outlook: if independent teams reproduce the reported gains across under-observed regions and build durable tools around the feeds, open observation layers could become as strategically important as open model weights in scientific AI. If access costs, regional bias, latency, or continuity limit reuse, the release will remain valuable research infrastructure without becoming a dependable operational standard.

07 / What could change the picture

Risks & uncertainties

  • The performance results and release narrative come from the Google-led author team; the reviewed sources do not provide independent replication of the global accuracy claims.
  • Google says PARDIG is expected to be reliable where ground measurements are sparse, but that expectation is an inference from satellite-based training rather than direct validation in every region.
  • The roughly 10-kilometer grid and five-hour average latency can be unsuitable for hyperlocal or time-critical warnings.
  • Historical consistency can be affected by changing satellite coverage, sensors, upstream analyses, and processing methods.
  • Open licensing does not remove cloud-transfer, storage, compute, or operational-support costs.
  • A reusable data layer can propagate the same measurement and model biases into many downstream systems unless users compare it with local observations.
08 / Editorial analysis

The AI Signal take

The clever release is not another forecast chart. It is the plumbing beneath the chart. SatWeave turns a patchwork of satellites into one view; PARDIG turns sparse radar supervision into a dense rain record. Opening both gives outsiders something more useful than applause: material they can test, break, and repurpose.

09 / Provenance

Sources & publication record

The release post, paper, and developer documentation are primary Google or author-team sources. Performance claims remain author-reported and are described as such; no independent replication was found in the reviewed window.
